Hall IV · 19th – Early 20th Century

Transposition & Fractionation

Rearranging the message instead of replacing its letters

Transposition changes symbol positions. Fractionation splits symbols into coordinate components before rearranging them. These methods provide useful comparisons with modern permutations and mixing layers, while their historical variants retain exploitable structure.

19th Century WWI 3 Hard-to-Break Exhibits
🚂

Transposition vs Substitution: Substitution replaces letters (A→Q), preserving positions. Transposition keeps letters but moves them (position 1→7). Both methods preserve statistical information — substitution preserves frequency, transposition preserves frequency too. The letters are all still there. Only their arrangement changes.

Modern Connection

Comparing Historical Rearrangement with AES

ShiftRows in AES

AES ShiftRows rotates three rows of its byte-state array; the first row stays in place. It works with MixColumns and the other round operations to spread dependencies. This is a conceptual connection to permutation, not evidence of direct descent from columnar transposition.

Compare MixColumns with Fractionation

AES MixColumns is a linear transformation over GF(2⁸). It combines bytes within each column. Bifid instead rearranges coordinate components; the mechanisms are different, although both illustrate mixing.

Diffusion

Diffusion spreads the influence of input information across output components. A pure transposition only moves whole symbols and preserves their frequencies. AES combines permutation and linear mixing with nonlinear substitution and key addition.
