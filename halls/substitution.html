Hall II · Medieval — Renaissance · 800 – 1929

Classical Substitution

The arms race between letter-mappers and frequency counters

Fixed substitutions preserve recognizable structure. Al-Kindi’s surviving treatise explains frequency analysis; later homophonic, digraph and algebraic constructions change what patterns remain. Their attacks require different evidence and methods.

What This Hall Teaches

The Substitution Arms Race

Frequency Analysis

Al-Kindi's 850 AD technique. Monoalphabetic substitution preserves language-frequency patterns. With enough suitable ciphertext, frequency counts and word patterns can reveal the substitution; short messages need not have a unique solution. The Caesar cipher is a trivial special case.

Homophonic Defense

Assign multiple ciphertexts to common letters to flatten frequency peaks. A sophisticated attacker can still detect the homophonic system through structural patterns and solve it with enough ciphertext.

Digraph Encryption → AES Blocks

Playfair encrypts letter pairs; AES encrypts 128-bit blocks. Both operate on blocks, but this alone does not establish security. AES combines nonlinear and linear operations under a modern security model.

Matrix Multiplication → Modern Mixing

Hill’s cipher and AES MixColumns both mix multiple input symbols using linear algebra, but they use different fields and play different roles. A linear layer alone does not provide the nonlinear security of a complete modern block cipher.
