Medieval Period
~850 AD

Al-Kindi's Frequency Analysis

🇮🇶 Baghdad

Al-Kindi publishes A Manuscript on Deciphering Cryptographic Messages — the first known description of frequency analysis. Simple substitution exposes language patterns, although a short cryptogram may not determine a unique plaintext. The codebreakers lead for 600 years.

🌍 Arabic World

Monoalphabetic substitution — replace each letter with a fixed substitute. Foundation of all substitution ciphers; broken by Al-Kindi's frequency analysis ~850.

1640

Fermat's Little Theorem

🇫🇷 France

In a letter to Frénicle de Bessy, Pierre de Fermat states that aᵖ ≡ a (mod p) for every prime p — offered without proof ("I would send you the demonstration, did I not fear it being too long"). Three centuries later the off-hand remark becomes load-bearing: primality testing leans on it, and its generalization is the reason RSA decryption undoes encryption.

1643

🇬🇧 England

John Wallis served successive English governments as a cryptanalyst. The interactive illustrates a nomenclator rather than reproducing an authenticated Royalist key.

View exhibit →
1763

Euler's Theorem & the Totient

🇷🇺 St. Petersburg

Leonhard Euler generalizes Fermat's little theorem to composite moduli — aᵠ⁽ⁿ⁾ ≡ 1 (mod n) for a coprime to n — introducing the totient function φ along the way. In 1977 this identity becomes the correctness proof of RSA, the reason the private exponent undoes the public one, making a 1763 theorem one of the most-executed pieces of pure mathematics on Earth.

🇺🇸 United States

Claude Shannon publishes "Communication Theory of Secrecy Systems" (classified 1945, published 1949) — the mathematical foundation of modern cryptography. He formalizes perfect secrecy, including the correctly used one-time pad, and describes confusion and diffusion as cipher-design principles.

Modern Era
1976

Diffie-Hellman Key Exchange

🇺🇸 United States

Whitfield Diffie and Martin Hellman publish "New Directions in Cryptography" — inventing public-key cryptography. For the first time, two parties can agree on a secret key over an insecure channel without ever meeting. GCHQ had classified equivalent work by James Ellis years earlier.

1977

RSA & DES

🇺🇸 United States

Rivest, Shamir, and Adleman publish RSA — the first practical public-key cryptosystem. The same year, the National Bureau of Standards (NIST’s predecessor) standardizes DES as the federal encryption standard. The era of algorithmic, standardized cryptography begins.

1990

Kryptos at CIA Headquarters

🇺🇸 Langley, Virginia

Kryptos: K1–K3 publicly solved; K4 plaintext recovered from an archive in 2025, without a publicly demonstrated cryptanalytic solution.

View exhibit →
1994

Shor's Algorithm

🇺🇸 Bell Labs

Peter Shor gives polynomial-time quantum algorithms for factoring and discrete logarithms. A sufficiently large, fault-tolerant quantum computer could defeat RSA and conventional Diffie–Hellman and elliptic-curve systems. Post-quantum alternatives include lattice-based ML-KEM and ML-DSA and hash-based SLH-DSA, standardized in 2024.

View exhibit →
2001

AES Standardized

🇧🇪 Belgium / 🇺🇸 NIST

After a 5-year international competition, NIST selects Rijndael (by Joan Daemen and Vincent Rijmen) as the Advanced Encryption Standard. AES remains the backbone of symmetric encryption worldwide.

2005

SHA-1 Collision Attack

🇨🇳 China

Xiaoyun Wang and team demonstrate theoretical collision attacks against SHA-1, beginning the transition to SHA-256 and SHA-3. A practical collision is demonstrated by Google in 2017.

2005

Learning With Errors

🇮🇱 Tel Aviv University

Oded Regev introduces Learning With Errors: noisy modular equations hide a secret vector. His result connects suitable LWE parameters to worst-case lattice problems through a quantum reduction. Hardness depends on parameters; noise is not sufficient by itself. The later ML-KEM and ML-DSA standards use structured module problems, with additional assumptions for signatures.

View exhibit →
2013

Snowden Revelations

🌍 Global

Edward Snowden's disclosures reveal the scale of government surveillance. The revelations accelerate global adoption of end-to-end encryption — HTTPS becomes the default, messaging apps adopt Signal Protocol, and encryption moves from specialist tool to human right.

Quantum Era
2016–2024

Post-Quantum Cryptography

🌍 Global · NIST

NIST launches the Post-Quantum Cryptography project in 2016. In 2024, ML-KEM (FIPS 203), ML-DSA (FIPS 204), and SLH-DSA (FIPS 205) are standardized — designed to resist quantum computers running Shor's algorithm. The ancient arms race continues.
