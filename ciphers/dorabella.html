Symbol Inspector

Explore the 24 possible arc-and-orientation classes by selecting an orientation and arc count. This does not claim a solution; it visualizes the design space that makes short-text cryptanalysis hard.

Why This Matters

Elgar sent the cryptogram to Dora Penny on 14 July 1897. Its three lines are commonly transcribed as 87 symbols. The arc counts and orientations permit 24 symbol classes, but handwritten marks can be ambiguous. A proposed reading must explain the transcription and the decoding rules, not merely produce a plausible sentence.

Historical Context

David Kahn discusses the note in The Codebreakers: The Comprehensive History of Secret Communication from Ancient Times to the Internet, revised edition (1996), chapter 21. Later proposed readings disagree about its rules and content. Both English text and music have been investigated; neither hypothesis should be treated as established merely because Elgar was a composer.

How It Works

This inspector draws stylized semicircular arcs in eight orientations. It does not transcribe the complete manuscript or recover a key. In Dorabella Cipher as Musical Inspiration (2021; arXiv version deposited 2025), Bradley Hauer and colleagues compare transcriptions and use a combined 87-symbol reading. Their musical output is presented as a creative composition process, not an authenticated solution.

Why It Has Resisted
Why It Resists
Evidence: 87 commonly transcribed symbols

Frequency statistics depend on how the handwritten symbols are transcribed. An 87-symbol sample gives limited evidence, but its length alone neither rules out monoalphabetic substitution nor proves that a unique reading is impossible. A language-model score ranks candidates under a chosen model; it does not authenticate Elgar’s intended message.
