David Kahn

1930 – 2024

David Kahn was an American journalist and historian. The Codebreakers (1967; revised 1996) brought a broad history of cryptology to general readers through archival research and interviews. Its publication involved objections and changes requested by the NSA. His subsequent career included further books, founding editorial work on Cryptologia, and engagement with the institution that had objected to the manuscript.

Why This Person Matters

Kahn helped establish cryptologic history as a subject of public and scholarly study. The NSA’s recognition records his influence on people entering the field and his donation of rare research materials to the National Cryptologic Museum in 2010. He entered the NSA/CSS Cryptologic Hall of Honor in 2020. These are distinct events.

The Codebreakers

David Kahn’s The Codebreakers: The Comprehensive History of Secret Communication from Ancient Times to the Internet, revised edition (1996), is an important historical reference. Its account reflects the evidence available when it was written. Later archival releases and research can qualify particular claims, so it should be read alongside primary documents rather than treated as an infallible account.

The NSA and Suppression

The NSA objected to parts of the pre-publication manuscript. Some material was removed or modified after discussions; the book was not published wholly unchanged. It nevertheless made a large body of cryptologic history available to general readers. The surviving drafts and correspondence are evidence for this more complicated publication history.

Later Works

Kahn's subsequent books include: Hitler's Spies (1978), a comprehensive study of German military intelligence in WWII; Kahn on Codes (1983), a general-audience introduction; Seizing the Enigma (1991), a detailed account of US and British naval captures of Enigma material; and The Reader of Gentlemen's Mail (2004), a biography of Herbert Yardley. He died in January 2024 at age 93, after a long career documenting a field whose archives continued to open.

Evidence and Reading

NSA, “Dr. David Kahn”, identifies the 2020 induction and the separate 2010 library donation. The U.S. Naval Institute’s author biography records his Oxford history doctorate.
