Typed character codes are combined with a public deterministic sequence modulo six. This does not process speech, implement a vocoder or generate a secret random pad.

Why This Matters

SIGSALY was a pioneering secure digital voice system developed at Bell Laboratories. A vocoder represented speech by changing parameters, which were quantised and combined with matched key material. The one-time-pad principle required the random key values to remain secret and never be reused.

Context: Churchill-Roosevelt Communications

The earlier A-3 transatlantic speech scrambler was vulnerable to interception and analysis. Bell Laboratories developed SIGSALY for high-level Allied voice communications, and it entered service in 1943. The National Cryptologic Museum’s account describes forty equipment racks and substantial operating requirements. The vulnerability of A-3 and the deployment of SIGSALY are separate historical events.

How SIGSALY Worked

SIGSALY quantised vocoder parameters, including information about the speech spectrum and pitch, into discrete levels. The commonly quoted 50-per-second rate concerns these parameters, not direct sampling of an audio waveform at 50 Hz. Modular addition and subtraction with synchronised key records concealed and recovered the parameter stream.

Shannon's Foundation

Shannon’s wartime cryptographic work led to the published 1949 paper Communication Theory of Secrecy Systems. It formalised perfect secrecy and analysed key requirements. It did not prove that the one-time pad was the only possible perfectly secret cipher; his separate 1948 communication-theory paper should not be conflated with it.

Global Network

The NSA history records twelve terminals. The initial Pentagon–London service opened on 15 July 1943; later installations included Paris, North Africa, Hawaii, Guam, Manila and Australia. The number twelve refers to terminals, not rooms per installation. Each terminal needed substantial equipment and power, and the communicating ends needed matching, synchronised key records.
