McCormick Notes

Historical Only

Two handwritten notes found with Ricky McCormick in 1999. The FBI released them in 2011 seeking help; “unsolved” is not a declaration of mathematical unbreakability.

The FBI’s 2011 appeal reported the notes unresolved.

Why This Matters

Two handwritten notes were found with Ricky McCormick’s body in Missouri on 30 June 1999. The FBI’s appeal of 29 March 2011 reported unsuccessful analysis by its Cryptanalysis and Racketeering Records Unit and the American Cryptogram Association. It asked for new evidence or recognition of the notation. That dated account does not establish the notes’ contents or a cause of death.

Historical Context

In its 2011 appeal, the FBI attributed to family members the report that McCormick had used coded notes since childhood. The agency sought someone who might recognise the notation or provide context. The marks’ precise rules and meaning remain uncertain, so no invented quotation or particular algorithm is asserted here.

Technical Notes

Repeated clusters and layout offer clues, but they do not establish a unique cipher type or underlying language. Personal shorthand is one possibility. A private notation is not thereby a one-time pad: that term has specific requirements of random, secret, non-reused key material.

Modern Echo

The FBI sought another example of the notation or someone who recognized it. This illustrates the value of contextual evidence: the investigators did not possess a complete account of the writing system. Difficulty interpreting a private note is not proof of strong encryption or of an author’s technical ability.
