George Lasry

Classical cryptanalysis researcher

George Lasry researches the cryptanalysis of classical ciphers using computational search. His dissertation develops a methodology based on hill climbing and simulated annealing and evaluates it across several cipher systems and historical challenges. His collaborations combine statistical results with historical interpretation, including the 2023 decipherment of Mary Stuart’s letters with Norbert Biermann and Satoshi Tomokiyo.

Why This Person Matters

Lasry’s work illustrates how computer search, language statistics and historical interpretation can complement one another. A documented example is the recovery of Mary Stuart’s letters with Norbert Biermann and Satoshi Tomokiyo, published in 2023. This work should not be confused with the separate teams that solved Copiale in 2011 and Z340 in 2020.

The Copiale Cipher

The 2011 Copiale decipherment was the work of Kevin Knight, Beáta Megyesi and Christiane Schaefer, not Lasry. Their analysis found German plaintext: many ordinary Roman letters acted as nulls or separators, while the distinctive symbols carried the encrypted text. See their original paper.

Z340 and the Zodiac Ciphers

David Oranchak, Sam Blake and Jarl Van Eycke solved Z340 in 2020. That result is not attributed to Lasry here. His documented contributions to historical cryptanalysis include published work on classical cipher search and archival decipherment; see the Mary Stuart case below.

European Diplomatic Archives

In 2023 Lasry, Biermann and Tomokiyo published their decipherment of Mary Stuart’s lost letters from 1578–1584. The recovered correspondence demonstrates how identifying an archive’s unknown writer can be part of the cryptanalytic result.

Evidence and Reading

George Lasry, A Methodology for the Cryptanalysis of Classical Ciphers with Search Metaheuristics (Kassel University Press, 2018): title and degree page, abstract, and case studies. The degree page records the defence on 16 October 2017; the publication year is 2018.
