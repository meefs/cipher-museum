The GCHQ Trio — Ellis, Cocks, and Williamson

1970 – 1974

James Ellis’s classified report on the possibility of non-secret encryption is dated January 1970. Clifford Cocks proposed an RSA-related construction in 1973, and Malcolm Williamson developed finite-field key agreement in 1974. Their work remained classified while public academic research independently developed public-key cryptography. Public disclosure of the GCHQ work came in 1997.

Why This Person Matters

The episode distinguishes private discovery from publication and public adoption. Public-key methods became central to internet authentication and key establishment, but a mathematical proposal is not the same as a deployed, authenticated, securely encoded protocol. Counterfactual claims that earlier publication would have advanced the entire field by an exact number of years cannot be established.

James Ellis's Insight

Ellis’s January 1970 report, The Possibility of Secure Non-Secret Digital Encryption, argued that the idea was possible without supplying a practical construction. This date is the report’s date, distinct from accounts of his earlier thought process.

Clifford Cocks’s Construction

Cocks’s 1973 work used modular exponentiation with a composite modulus, closely related to the later RSA construction. Knowledge of the factors enables the needed private computation. It is not established that every possible attack on RSA inversion must factor the modulus, nor that Cocks’s formulation and every later RSA protocol were identical in every detail.

Recognition and Impact

The classified discoveries were disclosed in 1997, after independent public research had established public-key cryptography. Ellis died shortly before that disclosure. The IEEE dedicated a milestone to the GCHQ work in 2010. Cocks was appointed Companion of the Order of the Bath (CB) in 2008 and elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 2015.
