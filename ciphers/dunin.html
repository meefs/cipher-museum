Elonka Dunin

b. 1958

Elonka Dunin is a game developer, cryptography researcher and author. Her websites collect information about Kryptos and other unresolved cryptograms, and her books introduce classical cryptanalysis to general readers. Her work includes documentary research and public explanation as well as attempts at decipherment.

Why This Person Matters

Dunin’s work illustrates the importance of documenting evidence and making it accessible. Her Freedom of Information Act request helped bring NSA records about early Kryptos solutions into public view in 2013. A researcher’s archive can be useful without being an official certification of a cipher’s status; dates, sources and the meaning of “solved” still need checking.

Kryptos Research

Jim Sanborn’s Kryptos was installed at CIA headquarters in 1990. K1 and K2 use Vigenère-family substitutions; K3 uses transposition. Jim Gillogly publicly announced solutions in 1999, following earlier work at the NSA and David Stein’s independent CIA solution in 1998. Dunin’s documentation distinguishes these histories. The 2025 discovery of K4 plaintext material in Sanborn’s archive is a different achievement from reconstructing its encryption method; see the Kryptos exhibit for that distinction.

The Famous Unsolved Codes List

Her Famous Unsolved Codes and Ciphers list brings together puzzles with different kinds of uncertainty. An undeciphered language, an uncertain inscription and a known encryption system with a missing key are not the same problem. Such lists are starting points for research, and their historical entries must be checked against later discoveries.

Game Design and Cipher Narrative

Dunin worked in online game development at Simutronics. Her publications include The Mammoth Book of Secret Codes and Cryptograms (2006) and, with Klaus Schmeh, Codebreaking: A Practical Guide (2020). The latter presents techniques, worked cases and challenges, including both Gillogly’s IRA work and Bean’s later solution.

Evidence and Reading

Elonka Dunin and Klaus Schmeh, Codebreaking: A Practical Guide (Robinson, 2020), author biographies and IRA case studies. Dunin’s Kryptos archive and FAQ document her research and the K1–K3 solution history; older status statements in the FAQ need to be read in their original context.
