Latin American Telegraphic Codebooks

Why This Matters

Commercial telegraph codebooks replaced longer phrases with short codewords, potentially reducing charges under the applicable tariff. Savings varied by message and tariff; a universal 95 percent saving is not established. Published codebooks were not equivalent to modern confidential encryption.

Historical Context

Latin American trade formed part of expanding international telegraph networks. Specific operators, routes and dates require archival evidence; this exhibit does not establish that Western Union affiliates served every major port in a single period or that all books used the same code-group format.

Structure of a Commercial Codebook

Codebooks could contain business phrases, places, commodities and numbers. Editions varied widely, and possession of the same public book allowed lookup by an outsider. Private supplements or additional encipherment could change the security, so an edition number should not automatically be called a secret key.

Security Assessment
Codebook capture / economic intelligence
Complexity: Trivial with codebook in hand

Cost reduction and confidentiality are different objectives. A published book can make a message compact without concealing it from someone who has that book. The interactive’s vocabulary is invented for teaching; it is not a transcription of an identified Latin American commercial edition.
